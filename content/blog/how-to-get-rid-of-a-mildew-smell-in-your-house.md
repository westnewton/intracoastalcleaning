If you live near the coast, you know the smell: damp, earthy, a little stale. It hangs in a closet, rises from the bathroom, or greets you when you open a vacation home after a few weeks away. That's mildew—and in a humid climate like Wilmington's, it's one of the most common complaints we hear.

The good news: a musty smell is usually fixable without harsh chemicals. Here's how to find it, clean it, and keep it from coming back.

## What Causes That Musty Smell?

Mildew is a surface mold that grows wherever moisture, warmth, and still air meet. Coastal North Carolina supplies plenty of all three. The smell comes from compounds the mildew releases as it grows—so if you smell it, there's growth somewhere, even if you can't see it yet.

## Where to Look

Start with the places moisture collects:

- **Bathrooms:** shower grout and caulk, behind the toilet, under the sink, exhaust fan covers, and bath mats
- **Laundry:** the rubber door seal on front-loading washers, detergent drawers, and clothes left damp in the machine
- **Closets:** especially on exterior walls, where air doesn't circulate
- **Kitchen:** under the sink, the refrigerator drip pan, and dish racks
- **HVAC:** dirty filters, vents, and drain pans can spread odor through the whole house
- **Carpet and rugs:** particularly near sliding doors, entryways, and anywhere that's been wet
- **Window sills and tracks:** condensation collects there daily in humid months

Follow your nose. The smell is usually strongest within a few feet of the source.

## How to Clean Mildew Without Harsh Chemicals

You don't need bleach to deal with everyday mildew. These options work well on most household surfaces:

**White vinegar.** Spray undiluted vinegar on the affected area, let it sit for about an hour, then scrub and wipe clean. The vinegar smell fades as it dries.

**Baking soda.** Mix a tablespoon or two into a spray bottle of water, spray, scrub, and rinse. Baking soda also absorbs lingering odors—sprinkle it on carpets, let it sit, then vacuum.

**Hydrogen peroxide (3%).** Spray, let it sit for ten minutes, then scrub. Test it first on fabrics and colored grout, since it can lighten some surfaces.

A few safety notes: wear gloves, keep the room ventilated, and never mix cleaning products—especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar, which creates dangerous fumes.

## When It's More Than Mildew

Surface mildew is a cleaning job. Larger mold problems are not. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency advises that if mold covers more than about 10 square feet, or follows flooding or a water leak, you should consult its mold cleanup guidance or a professional remediation company. The same goes for mold inside walls, under flooring, or in HVAC ductwork, and for anyone in the home with asthma or a weakened immune system.

## How to Keep the Smell From Coming Back

Cleaning removes the growth. Controlling moisture keeps it away:

- **Manage humidity.** The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60%, ideally between 30% and 50%. A simple hygrometer tells you where you stand; a dehumidifier helps in closets, basements, and closed-up rooms.
- **Run the exhaust fan** during showers and for at least 20 minutes after.
- **Leave the washer door open** between loads and wipe the door seal weekly.
- **Change HVAC filters** on schedule, and keep the system running in vacation homes between stays.
- **Let air move.** Leave a little space between furniture and exterior walls, and keep closet doors cracked in humid months.
- **Clean regularly.** Mildew is much easier to stop on a bathroom that's cleaned weekly than one that's cleaned monthly.
